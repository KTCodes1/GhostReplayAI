# Ghost Replay: validation results

Generated by `npm run validate` on 2026-10-26. Held-out synthetic matches (seeds 101, 102, 103, 104, 105, 106, 107, 108), never used while tuning: **12,189 judged decisions**.

> **What this does and doesn't show.** Every match is synthetic, so these tests show that the engine is *consistent with its own simulated world* and *behaves sensibly when the situation changes*. They do not show accuracy against real football, which would need real tracking data in the same format.

## 1. Calibration: do predicted chances match what happens?

| 40–50% | 200 | 45.2% | 52.0% | +6.8 pts |

The pass model is calibrated with Platt scaling fitted on **training** matches only (seeds 1–12, `npm run fit-calibration -w @ghost/engine`), so these held-out numbers are a fair test of it.

### 1b. Reactive simulation against the model (options not taken)

For alternative passes the player did *not* play, the model's chance is compared with how often the pass comes off when the 50-run simulation plays it with defenders reacting.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30–40% | 10 | 36.1% | 19.0% | -17.1 pts |
| 40–50% | 21 | 45.2% | 23.1% | -22.1 pts |

| 60–70% | 49 | 64.9% | 46.5% | -18.4 pts |

| 90–100% | 44 | 92.6% | 95.2% | +2.6 pts |

**308 options, 20 runs each. Expected calibration error 11.5%.** The simulation is a separate physical model (reaction time, acceleration, reach, a race for the ball), so this checks the two agree; both are still models.

**Reading this:** for confident options (80%+) the two agree within a few points. In the middle range the simulation is noticeably *stricter* than the model: its race for the ball at the receiver favours defenders more than real match outcomes do. That makes the simulation check conservative: a better option has to clear a harder bar to be shown. Closing this gap is listed under Known gaps.

## 2. Counterfactual robustness: change one thing, does the verdict move sensibly?

For every flagged moment whose better option is a pass, we change one thing in the freeze frame (or the players), keep everything else fixed, and re-judge.

| Change | Expected | Moved as expected | Unchanged | Moved the wrong way |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Move the defender nearest to where the ball arrives 2 m closer to that spot | chance of the pass coming off goes down | 100 of 121 (82.6%) | 12 | 9 |
| Move that defender 4 m further from the spot | chance goes up (or stays, if the spot was already free) | 95 of 121 (78.5%) | 15 | 11 |
| Put a defender in the middle of the passing lane | chance goes down | 119 of 121 (98.3%) | 0 | 2 |
| Defender moved closer: does the option lose value against the others? | its lead over the next-best option shrinks | 98 of 121 (81.0%) | 0 | 23 |
| Same pass simulated against slow vs fast defenders (pace 20 vs 95) | it comes off at least as often against slow defenders | 87 of 121 (71.9%) | 34 | 0 |

"Unchanged" is expected when the change can't matter: a receiver already 5 m+ from everyone can't get freer, and a defender too far from a pass can't reach it whatever their pace.

## 3. Explanation correctness: does the evidence checker catch false claims?

For every moment (live and recap), the checker gets the engine's fact sheet plus a deliberately false sentence of each kind below. A claim counts as **caught** if the checker reports a problem.

**Example.** Fact: *"Nearest defender to Moreau: Zielonka, 1.4 m away."* False claim: *"Moreau was completely unmarked."* Checker: *"it says Moreau was unmarked, but a defender was 1.4 m away"*

| False claim | Caught |
| --- | --- |
| An invented percentage (one that appears nowhere in the facts) | 125 of 125 (100.0%) |
| A wrong distance (2.5 m off the real one) | 87 of 98 (88.8%) |
| A player who isn't part of the moment | 125 of 125 (100.0%) |
| "Unmarked" when a defender was under 3 m away | 85 of 85 (100.0%) |
| "Could see" an option the facts say was outside the modeled view | 96 of 96 (100.0%) |
| An offside nobody was in | 98 of 98 (100.0%) |
| A claim citing a fact that doesn't exist | 125 of 125 (100.0%) |

**Explanations written from the facts pass the same check 125 of 125 times (100.0%)**, so the checker doesn't reject true statements built from the facts.

## 4. The simulation check: how often does it uphold the model?

**Definition.** A moment is *flagged* when the model's estimate of value left on the table passes the detector's bar (and the rate limits allow it). It is *upheld* when the 50-run simulation, with defenders reacting, finds that one of the top three fair alternatives creates at least 0.004 more expected goals per attempt than the choice the player made. If no alternative does, the moment is dropped: the simulation decides the model's estimate wasn't backed up, which is **not** proof that the player was right, only that the gain isn't clear.

**Result: 119 of 221 flagged moments upheld (53.8%); 102 dropped before they reached the screen.**

**Example (seed 101, minute 5).** Ansomah chose shot; the model flagged carry as worth 0.108 more. In 50 runs it created 0.405 expected goals per attempt against 0.193, so it was upheld (confidence: high).

## Known gaps

- **Model vs simulation in the middle range.** Options the model rates 35–80% come off less often in the reactive simulation (see 1b). The simulation is conservative, so this can only drop moments, never invent them, but the two models aren't yet reconciled.
- **Wrong distances.** The checker caught 87 of 98. It ties a distance to the player named in the sentence and allows 0.5 m for rounding, so a wrong number that happens to match another distance about the same player gets through.
- **Synthetic world.** Calibration is against outcomes in simulated matches. Real-world accuracy needs real tracking data in the same shape; the models are built to take it.
- **Modeled vision.** Visibility is a geometric estimate of field of view from movement and the last pass, not a measure of where the player actually looked. With no movement information it is labelled *unknown* rather than guessed.
